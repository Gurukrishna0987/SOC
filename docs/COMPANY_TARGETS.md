# Where to apply — India, SOC + VAPT, 2026–2027

**The rule:** apply where the *volume* of entry-level security hiring is, and
where you can show real work. Do not only chase brand names.

---

| **Consulting / audit** | Deloitte, KPMG, EY, PwC | Cyber risk and GRC analyst roles; strong training; GRC is a less crowded entry point than SOC and pivots to SOC later. |

**How to apply:** set up alerts on `nabard.org`, `cert-in.org.in`, `rbi.org.in`
and PSU career pages, plus the Telegram/WhatsApp job groups for government
notifications. Government postings close in 10–20 days — check the calendar
weekly.

## The tiering that actually matters

Do not spread 60 applications across 60 companies. Spread them like this:

| Bucket | Companies | Applications | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | 6 Tier-A firms you would genuinely join | 18 (3 each) | Highest probability; apply first, apply thoroughly |
| **A2** | 8 Tier-B SMEs/startups | 16 (2 each) | Fastest response, real work, best conversion odds |
| **A3** | 4 GCCs / product companies in your city | 12 (3 each) | Best brand on your CV, good fresher pipeline |
| **B** | Government / PSU / bank notifications | 8 | High value, low volume, time-sensitive |
| **C** | Long shot (global product firms, dream roles) | 6 | Cheap to apply, occasionally works |
| **D** | Part-time / WFH internships | 4 | Insurance: guarantees you have *something* by December |

**Add one "insurance" internship in the first two weeks of December** so you
are never at zero. Then keep replacing it with something better.

## The three-day research routine

Spend 3 hours per week on this and your applications get 3× sharper.

1. **Pick a company. Read its last 5 job posts.** Not just the title — read
   the JD and note the exact tools (Splunk vs Sentinel vs Elastic), the
   shift pattern, and whether it mentions "pre-placement" or "full-time
   offer after internship".
2. **Find the hiring manager on LinkedIn.** Search "[Company] + SOC Lead" or
   "[Company] + Cyber Security Manager". Send them the cold email. Same
   email, one line changed to name their team.
3. **Find one engineer from that team and one recruiter.** Message them
   separately. Recruiters tell you the *real* requirement ("we need someone
   who can write SPL, not just read dashboards"), and engineers tell you
   what the job is actually like. Both are gold.

## How to spot a good internship before you accept

Ask these questions in the call. A good SOC internship answers them clearly
and honestly.

| Question | What a good answer sounds like |
|---|---|
| "What does a typical shift look like for an intern?" | "You monitor the queue for 4 hours, we do a handover briefing, you'll triage with a mentor reviewing every ticket for the first month." |
| "Will I write any detection content?" | "Yes, once you're through the first two weeks we'll give you one alert type to tune." |
| "Is there a pre-placement offer?" | "Yes, we convert interns who complete 6 months and hit our evaluation criteria." If they dodge it, assume no. |
| "What are the shift timings and how many nights per month?" | A specific number, honestly. If the answer is "it changes", ask for the average. |
| "Who reviews my tickets?" | A named person or role. If nobody reviews them, you will be a noise filter, not a trainee. |
| "What tools do you run?" | Splunk, Sentinel, Elastic, QRadar, etc. — ask for the version and whether there is a free tier or training budget. |
| "Is training or certification funded?" | Many firms pay for Security+ or a Splunk cert. If yes, that is a strong signal you are valued. |

**Red flags:** "you will do whatever the team needs" with no training plan;
unpaid internship with a 6-hour "learning" day; no named mentor; a "SOC" role
that is really a data-entry job with a security title. Ask directly — a
fresher's time is the cheapest thing an employer can waste.
